Data Strategy · explained in plain language

The Tax Answer Factory

Every sale needs one thing from us: the right amount of tax, for the right place, at the right moment — and proof we got it right if anyone ever asks. This is how we build the "factory" that produces that answer, over and over, without slipping up.

The one big idea

Think of tax data like a factory that makes correct answers

Raw information goes in one end. A correct, provable tax answer comes out the other. In between, everything is checked, dated, and recorded — so we can always show our work.

Tax is unusually unforgiving. Rates and rules change thousands of times a year, there are more than 13,000 tax areas in the US alone, and a wrong answer can mean fines years later. So the factory is designed around three promises: the answer is right, it's provable, and it can be re-created exactly — even years from now.

How the factory works

The journey of a piece of data

Information doesn't jump straight to "usable." It moves down an assembly line, getting cleaner and more trustworthy at each stop — like ingredients becoming a finished dish.

Delivered — new rates, rules, and sales land exactly as they arrived (kept forever, untouched). Cleaned — duplicates removed, formats fixed, everything stamped with dates. Ready — organized into the shapes each team actually needs. On the shelf — the most-used facts kept somewhere lightning-fast for checkout.

What it's all for

The jobs this data has to do

Different teams ask the factory for different things — some need answers in the blink of an eye, others once a month.

Charge tax instantly

At checkout, tell the customer the exact tax in under the blink of an eye.

File the paperwork

Add up what was collected and send the right forms to each tax office on time.

Handle exemptions

Track which customers don't pay tax (and their valid certificates) so we don't over-charge.

Know where we owe

Watch sales in each region so we register to collect tax the moment we're required to.

Survive an audit

Re-create any past answer exactly, with a full paper trail of why it was right.

Work across borders

Handle VAT/GST and different rules country by country, not just US sales tax.

Jargon, translated

The technical words — in plain English

If you ever see these terms in the detailed plan, here's what they actually mean.

The technical term
What it really means
Tax content
The rulebook — every tax rate and rule, which changes all the time.
Determination
The answer — how much tax to charge on this sale.
Bitemporal
A time machine — we remember what the rules were on any past date, not just today's rules.
Immutable ledger

An inspector — data can't pass until it clears the checks.
Data product
A well-kept dataset with a named owner, like a labeled shelf someone is responsible for.

The three promises

What the factory guarantees

1

The answer is right

Rules are updated before they take effect, and a set of test sales runs on every update to catch mistakes — like a "70%" tax rate that should have been "7%".

2

The answer is provable

Every tax charge keeps its receipts: what we knew, which rule we used, and when. If an auditor asks "why this number?", we can show exactly why.

3

The answer can be re-created

Years later, we can rewind the time machine and reproduce any past answer perfectly — using the rules that were in force back then, not today's.

Why we're this careful: in most software a slow answer is the worst outcome. In tax, a wrong answer is worse — it can surface as a fine years later. So when the factory is ever unsure, it plays it safe and flags it, rather than quietly guessing.
